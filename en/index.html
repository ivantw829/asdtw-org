Formosa Autistic Commune

A mutual aid and advocacy community for disabled and LGBTQ+ people

We are a Discord-based community founded on July 31, 2026, with over 440 members. We welcome disabled people with any degree of disability and people of all genders and sexual orientations, and we also take part in social movements on the ground.

Who We Are

Formosa Autistic Commune is a mutual aid and advocacy community centered on disabled and LGBTQ+ people, committed to diverse and progressive values.

1. Centered on Disabled and LGBTQ+ People

Our community is open to everyone, but what we discuss, how we speak publicly, and where we are headed all center on the experiences of the people directly affected.

2. Nothing About Us Without Us

This slogan of the disability rights movement is our most fundamental principle. In any policy, research, service, or media coverage concerning disabled and LGBTQ+ people, the people directly affected must be involved from the beginning—not merely consulted at the end, and never spoken for by others. In Taiwan, public conversations about autism are often dominated by parents, clinicians, and special education professionals; we want autistic people’s own voices to be heard.

3. Against Ableism

We oppose ableism and understand disability and gender through the lens of critical sociology. Ableism establishes a specific set of bodily and mental states as the norm and defines those who diverge from it as defective individuals in need of correction. Adopting the social model of disability, we distinguish between individual impairment and the disability that society creates, maintaining that disability stems from exclusionary institutions, environments, and attitudes rather than individual bodies or minds. Norms around gender and sexuality are likewise socially constructed; heteronormativity, cisnormativity, and able-bodied norms reinforce one another to decide who counts as “normal.”

4. Committed to Praxis

We value praxis. Theory must be tested in action, and action requires theoretical reflection. That is why we take to the streets and encourage members to write and publish articles, turning lived experience into public discourse.
