Formosa Autistic Commune

A mutual aid and advocacy community for disabled people and gender/sexual minorities

We are a community based on Discord, founded on July 31, 2026, with over 440 members. We welcome disabled people of all support needs and people of all genders and sexual orientations, and we also take part in offline social movements.

Who We Are

Formosa Autistic Commune is a mutual aid and advocacy community centered on disabled people and gender/sexual minorities, committed to pluralistic and progressive values.

1. Centered on Disabled People and Gender/Sexual Minorities

Our community is open to everyone, but the issues we discuss, how we speak publicly, and the direction we take are all grounded in lived experience.

2. Nothing About Us Without Us

This slogan of the disability rights movement is our most fundamental principle. In any policy, research, service, or media coverage concerning disabled people and gender/sexual minorities, the people directly affected must be involved from the beginning—not merely consulted at the end, and never spoken for by others. In Taiwan, public conversations about autism are often dominated by parents, clinicians, and special education professionals; we are here to make sure autistic people’s own voices are heard.

3. Against Ableism

We oppose ableism and understand disability and gender through the lens of critical sociology. Ableism establishes a specific set of bodily and mental states as the norm and defines those who diverge from it as defective individuals in need of correction. Adopting the social model of disability, we distinguish between personal impairment and socially constructed disability, maintaining that disability stems from exclusionary institutions, environments, and attitudes rather than individual bodies or minds. Norms around gender and sexuality are likewise socially constructed; heteronormativity, cisnormativity, and able-bodied norms reinforce one another to decide who counts as “normal.”

4. Committed to Praxis

We value praxis. Theory must be tested in action, and action requires theoretical reflection. That is why we take to the streets and encourage members to write and publish articles, turning lived experience into public discourse.
